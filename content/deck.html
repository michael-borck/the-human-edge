JUDGE · THE WIDER MAP, IN 90 SECONDS
GenAI is the slice you can talk to. It is not the whole pie.
AI — PERCEIVE · PREDICT · RECOMMEND · GENERATE
Machine learning — learns patterns from examples
Neural networks / deep learning — layered pattern learning
Generative AI — creates text, image, audio, code
Large language models — today's working surface

NLP (natural language processing) is the language-task field that cuts across these layers, from classification to conversation.

THE OUTER PIE IS ALREADY AT WORK

Spam filters, search ranking, fraud alerts, recommendations, demand forecasts, transcription, image recognition.

WHY TODAY STARTS WITH GENAI

Natural language became the interface. No code, no specialist team: anyone can direct work, inspect output, and redesign a process from a browser.

The judgement travels. The prompts are the local dialect. Predictive AI is governed through data quality, false positives, bias, and drift. GenAI adds a new hazard: fluent artefacts that can look finished before they are right.

Ninety seconds, no more. Walk outside in. Most AI in their organisations is not a chatbot: it ranks, flags, forecasts, and classifies quietly. Machine learning learns from examples; neural networks are one powerful way to do that; generative models create new content; LLMs are the language-centred members of that family. Modern assistants wrap an LLM with files, search, memory, code, and tools, so do not reduce the whole product to next-token prediction. Scope line to say verbatim: when I say AI today, I usually mean GenAI assistants, because conversation puts them on every desk. The Trust Tool travels to the wider pie; RTCF does not retrain a fraud model.
JUDGE · YOUR ALARM
AI's mistakes don't look like mistakes.
WEAK HUMAN WORK ANNOUNCES ITSELF

Sloppy, gapped, rushed. Your alarm fires. You check.

A lifetime of calibration — for people.

WEAK AI WORK ANNOUNCES NOTHING

Fluent, complete, confident — a convincing average: the most plausible typical answer, whether or not it is right.

Your alarm stays quiet exactly when you need it most.

A GenAI assistant can generate polished output without reliably knowing when it is wrong. And watch the second trap, Gell-Mann amnesia: you catch its errors in your own domain, then trust it on the market, the rules, the numbers, the bits you cannot check. Stay most sceptical where you know least.

Merge of the old four slides (good at / bad at / hallucination / convincing average) into one contrast. Pattern tasks are strong. Unchecked precision, source-free facts, hidden calculation steps, missing context, and ethical calls are not. Modern assistants can hedge, search, and use code, which changes the error profile without removing the need to verify. Land it and move to the tool.

BRIEF · CONVERSATION, NOT DELEGATION
Brief a capable colleague. Don't cast a spell.

There are no magic words. Everything you would tell a smart new hire before they drafted for you — who it is for, what happened, what good looks like — belongs in the prompt.

The tool supplies the form. You supply the substance: intent, context, standard.

Most vague output is a vague brief, not a weak tool. RTCF makes the brief repeatable.
BRIEF · FRAMEWORK 2 — RTCF
Role. Task. Context. Format.
R — ROLE

Who should it be?

"Senior project manager, direct and empathetic." Specific beats theatrical.

T — TASK

What exactly?

Verb + scope: "Write a 200-word client email explaining a two-week delay."

C — CONTEXT · ONLY YOU CAN SUPPLY THIS

What must it know?

Audience, history, stakes, constraints. Add a stakes line: "for someone deciding this week who cannot undo it."

F — FORMAT

What shape comes back?

Length, structure, table columns — output you can paste, not rewrite.

Not every prompt needs all four. Anything important does.

Context is the component the tool genuinely cannot supply itself, and the one most often skipped. Live demo next slide.
BRIEF · BEFORE / AFTER — LIVE DEMO
Sixty seconds of briefing saves thirty of rewriting.

WITH RTCF

Close to sendable. The difference is the brief, not the model.

BRIEF · WHEN THE MODEL IS WEAKER
Weak models can't infer. So leave nothing to infer.
  • 1. Full RTCF, every time — strong models forgive a sloppy brief; weaker (and free-tier) models cannot. The weaker the model, the more RTCF earns.
  • 2. Show, don't describe — paste one good example of the output you want. One example beats three adjectives.
  • 3. Constrain explicitly — "Use only the figures in the table. If a figure is missing, write MISSING — do not estimate."
  • 4. One step at a time — split the job; check each step before the next. Ask it to show its working.
  • 5. Prompt past the flattery (fact-gate pushback) — the first trap has a name too: sycophancy. Models praise first and fold when pushed, even when they were right. So gate the fold: — "Only change your assessment if I give you new facts, and name the fact that changed it." Then push with no evidence and watch whether it folds.
This ladder is new. Frame it as insurance that also improves strong-model output: examples, constraints, decomposition, and fact-gating help every model — they are simply non-negotiable on a weaker one. Never teach "the model will just know."

VERIFY · DATA WORK
A number that drives a decision is never "about right."

AI finds patterns fast. It also miscounts, confuses correlation with cause, ignores missing data silently, and invents figures that were never in the source.

Fluency is not evidence. The check comes from outside the tool, against the data, the source, the standard. The method has a name: VET it.

  • Verify the source. Can you find it independently of the chat?
  • Explain it back. Make it unpack the claim until you could explain it yourself.
  • Test the edges. What case, cut, or counterexample would make it wrong?
Asking the model "are you sure?" is not verification; it reaffirms itself. Start analysis in a fresh chat — long conversations drift toward your framing.

VERIFY · THE VERDICTS
Fluent, professional, plausible — and wrong.
CLAIM A — VERIFIED

"Email ~3x slower than live chat." True: 13.2h vs 4.0h = 3.3x.

CLAIM B — REFUTED TWICE

"Account Issues: lowest satisfaction, most complex." False both ways: Returns & Refunds is lowest; Account Issues resolves best (~96%). Forward this and Karen fixes the wrong thing.

CLAIM C — OVERSTATED

"Sundays more than double." 1.5x, not 2x (13.7h vs 9.2h). Direction right, staffing number wrong. Precision quadrant: close is wrong.

Patterns to find: Returns & Refunds worst, Delivery next, Sundays/Mondays and December blow out, Email is the slow channel.

Vote per claim before revealing each verdict. Claim B is the lesson of the day: AI failure does not look like nonsense, it looks like insight. The substance — the check — was yours.

REDESIGN · AGENTS AND SKILLS
An agent automates the loop. Not the standard.
  • Agent — a model in a loop with tools: drafts, checks, calls, retries until done
  • Harness — the software around it: standing instructions, context it can read, tools it may call
  • Skill — the part you own: a plain-text brief the harness loads on match. Your RTCF brief, format, and verification rule, written once

A loop that checks its own work inherits the blind spot you saw in Exercise 3. The standard still comes from outside.

Durable version, no hype. Everything built today — brief, format, verification step — is exactly what belongs in a skill file. Optional 3-minute demo kit lives in instructor materials; the idea matters more than the demo.
