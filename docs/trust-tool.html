The trust tool

Two questions that turn "can I trust this?" into an answer

AI produces a convincing average: fluent, well-shaped, usually about right. Whether that is good enough depends on what you need and what is at stake. Place the output on a 2×2 grid and the review it needs falls out of the corner you land in.

The two questions

Question one: do you need an average, or precision? An average answer is one where "in the right ballpark" is genuinely fine: a first draft, a summary, a starting point you will shape further. A precise answer is one where there is a single correct value and being close is the same as being wrong: a figure in a contract, a citation, a customer's account balance.

Question two: are the stakes small or large? A small decision is easy to reverse and cheap to get wrong. A large decision carries real consequences: money, safety, reputation, someone's job or wellbeing.

The four corners

Small (low stakes) Large (high stakes)
Average is fine 🟢 Trust with light review. Drafts, brainstorming, first-pass summaries, language polish. Act on it, skim later. 🟡 Trust, but verify the load-bearing bits. The framing matters even when the prose is average. Name the claims the decision hangs on, and check those.
Precision required 🟡 Verify before you ship. A calculation, a citation, a fact you will quote. Small blast radius if wrong, but still wrong. 🔴 Human in the loop, non-negotiable. Anything that touches a person, money, safety, or a decision you cannot walk back.

How to use it

For any AI output you are about to use, place it on the grid and do what the corner says. Most AI failures reported in the news are red-corner outputs that were treated as though they were green. Placing the output on the grid before acting makes that error visible while it is still cheap to fix.

One rule of thumb to remember: lean in where "about right" over low stakes is fine; keep a human in the loop where exactly-right meets high stakes.

Capability moves the boundary

A frontier model and a small local model can both produce strong and weak outputs. Better models shift where the boundary tends to sit, and they demand less checking on easy ground. They never remove your obligation to place the output on the grid: capability raises the floor, it does not remove the review. That is what makes the tool durable. It works on any platform, and it does not expire when the next model ships.
