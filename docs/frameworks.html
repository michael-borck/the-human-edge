The trust tool (the spine)

Question: How much should I trust this AI output?

AI produces a convincing average: fluent, well-shaped, usually about right. Whether that's good enough depends on two questions:

Q1 Do you need an average, or precision? (Is "in the ballpark" fine, or is close the same as wrong?) Q2 Are the stakes small, or large? (Easy to reverse, or real consequences?)
Small (low stakes) Large (high stakes)
Average is fine Lean in. Let AI run. Lean in, with a glance. Sanity-check before it goes out.
Precision required Use, then verify. Confirm the specifics. Human in the loop. A person owns the decision.

Key principle: Verification comes from outside the tool, the model can't reliably check its own work. Your judgement is the edge.

Used in: Every exercise, all day

AI task suitability spectrum

Question: WHEN should I use AI for this task?

AI-Led ◄──────────────────────────────► Human-Only Routine Judgment Complex Empathy High-volume AI-supported Human-led Relationships Pattern-based Editing/review Sub-task help Novel judgment

Four zones

  • AI-Led: Drafting, summarising, formatting, data entry
  • AI-Assisted: Analysis, research, editing, brainstorming
  • Human-Led, AI-Supported: Strategy, design, negotiation prep
  • Human-Only: Performance reviews, crisis leadership, creative vision

RTCF prompt framework

Question: HOW do I brief AI effectively?

R, Role Set the AI's expertise and perspective T, Task Define the specific action clearly C, Context Background and constraints, the grounding only you can supply F, Format Specify the desired output structure

Key principle: Conversation, not delegation. Brief it like a capable junior colleague, the tool returns the form of the work; you supply the substance.

Practised in: Exercise 2, Prompt Engineering Workshop (11:30 AM)

AI-assisted analysis workflow

Question: How do I use AI for DATA work?

Prepare → Upload → Question → Verify → Refine → Present ▲ MOST IMPORTANT

Six steps

  1. Prepare: Define the question first; start a fresh chat (long chats drift toward your view)
  2. Upload: Get data into the AI appropriately
  3. Question: Ask progressively deeper questions
  4. Verify: Check claims against the data, from outside the tool
  5. Refine: Iterate based on findings
  6. Present: Format for your audience

Key principle: AI finds patterns fast, but only an external check tells a real pattern from a convincing fake.

Practised in: Exercise 3, Data Analysis Challenge (1:45 PM)
