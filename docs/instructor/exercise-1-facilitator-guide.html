Exercise 1. The compare drill

Loop 1, Judge. 35 minutes. Workbook Exercise 1.

This replaces the old test drive where people ran one prompt through three tools and compared the tools. The tools have mostly converged, and ranking them teaches shopping. Comparing the AI against your own first draft teaches judgement, and judgement transfers when the tools change. I would rather they leave with that.

Run it

Put the task on screen and nothing else. A strong performer of three years has been late and missing deadlines for a month. The team has noticed. Sketch your plan to address it.

Give them five silent minutes to write their own pass in the workbook. Hold this line. If they open the AI first there is nothing to compare and the exercise turns into tool play.

Then the same task, word for word, into their AI tool. Ten minutes. They paste or summarise what came back.

Then pairs compare for ten minutes. Where did you beat it. Where did it beat you. What did it say that sounds fine and is wrong. They put the task on the Suitability Spectrum and the output on the Trust Tool before the debrief starts.

Debrief

Pull out three or four real gaps from the room. If someone got bad or tone deaf advice, stay on it a while. That is the lesson, not an embarrassment to skip past.

Three points have to land. An average was fine for the structure. Only the humans in the room knew the history and what a good outcome looks like here. And almost nobody checked anything the AI said, which is exactly what the afternoon is for.

Name what they just did. This is the compare drill. One task that matters, your pass, the AI pass, side by side. It takes minutes and it is the habit worth keeping.

Watch for two things. People who skipped their own pass need a quiet reset, not a telling off. Anyone still locked out of an AI account needs a partner or a spare laptop now, because every exercise after this one assumes access.

Bridge to the break with one line. That was a basic prompt. Next loop we fix the brief, and it is less about clever wording than most people expect.
